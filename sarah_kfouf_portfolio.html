How I actually work

I'm a fresh Computer Science graduate, but I've spent the last two years leading — a 70-hour robotics program, a 5-person graduation capstone, a national AI hackathon final, and a healthcare business case that ran a full feasibility study from scratch. Every one of those experiences taught me the same lesson from a different angle.

Technical skill gets a project started. What actually finishes it — on time, on budget, with a team that still wants to work with you afterward — is reading people accurately: knowing who needs clear instructions and who needs ownership, who thrives under pressure and who needs a calmer hand, and adjusting in real time without losing the plan.
The same instinct shows up in how I think about products. I don't ask "does this work technically" first — I ask who is this for, what happens when it breaks, and does the business actually survive on this decision. That question has shaped every project below, including a national robotics championship where I led a technically diverse team through 70+ hours of hands-on training and a full hardware build under real competition pressure.
I'm technically fluent enough to work shoulder-to-shoulder with developers, designers, and data scientists — full-stack development, UI/UX, AI automation, machine learning, security fundamentals — but my seat at the table is the one asking whether it should be built this way, not just whether it can be.

CS-02 · Business Analyst

AI-Healthcare System — Elderly & Chronic Care Monitoring

Systems Analysis & Design Project · The Hashemite University · 2025 · Team of 4
Full Business Case Delivered

This one wasn't a coding project — it was a full business case, start to finish, for a voice-first AI monitoring system built for elderly and chronically ill patients who often can't operate a smartphone or read a screen. The premise mattered to me: most "smart" healthcare tools are designed for people who are comfortable with technology, which quietly excludes the people who need them most.

My team split the work evenly across every phase — I contributed across the Project Scope Statement, the feasibility study, and requirements gathering. We ran stakeholder interviews with an elderly patient, a caregiver, a doctor, an emergency responder, and a system administrator; distributed a survey to 10+ respondents; and ran a full Joint Application Development (JAD) session to pressure-test the requirements with everyone in the room at once.

The business case had to hold up financially, not just technically. We modeled a $44,500 one-time build cost against $65,000/year in projected benefits (reduced emergency response time, reduced staffing cost, better clinical data) — landing at a 5-year NPV of $217,890 and a break-even point of roughly 1 year and 2 months. We also mapped the full requirement set into use case diagrams and data flow diagrams (DFDs), and stress-tested our own solution against four rejected alternatives before defending our final recommendation.

  • Full feasibility study — cost/benefit, NPV, ROI, break-even analysis
  • Stakeholder interviews, 10+ respondent survey, and a JAD session
  • Use case & data flow diagrams translating research into system requirements
  • Evaluated 4 alternative solutions before recommending the final design

CS-03 · Co-Lead

IQ-Think — Smart AI-Powered Energy Monitoring System

2-Person Team · The Hashemite University · 2025
Finalist — Top team of 60+

Most smart-energy pitches lead with sensors and dashboards. Ours led with a harder question: what happens to an elderly, disabled, or low-tech user when the "smart" system assumes everyone can read a chart? IQ-Think was built around that gap — a human-centered IoT/AI energy platform with multilingual voice control, accessible interfaces, and an AI layer for consumption forecasting and anomaly detection.

Co-leading a 2-person team, I built the live monitoring dashboard on ThingsBoard, integrating an ESP32 microcontroller, current and voltage sensors (SCT-013, INA219), and a Raspberry Pi 4 running Docker-hosted MQTT services for real-time data. I also independently designed and built the full proposal and documentation site presenting our architecture and business case to judges.

We framed the pitch around adoption, not just technical ambition — projecting a 10–30% reduction in energy costs and prioritizing onboarding simplicity for exactly the users most existing platforms leave behind. It advanced us to the final stage among more than 60 competing teams.

  • Built live IoT dashboard: ESP32, SCT-013 & INA219 sensors, Raspberry Pi 4, Docker/MQTT
  • Designed & built the full proposal + documentation website solo
  • Directed the accessibility-first, multilingual product concept
  • Projected 10–30% energy cost reduction in the business case
